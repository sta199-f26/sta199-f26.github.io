# Presentation + slides

## Slides

In addition to the written report, your team will also create presentation slides and deliver a presentation that summarize and showcase your project. Introduce your research question and dataset, showcase visualizations, and discuss the primary conclusions. These slides should serve as a brief visual addition to your written report and will be graded for content and quality.

You can create your slides with any software you like (Keynote, PowerPoint, Google Slides, etc.). We recommend choosing an option that’s easy to collaborate with, e.g., Google Slides. If you choose this option, save the slides as a PDF and upload it to your repo as `presentation.pdf`.

You can also use Quarto to make your slides! While we won’t be covering making slides with Quarto in the class, we would be happy to help you with it in office hours. It’s no different than writing other documents with Quarto, so the learning curve will not be steep!

The slide deck should be roughly 6 content slides + 1 title slide. Here is a *suggested* outline as you think through the slides; you ***do not*** have to use this exact format for the 6 slides.

- Title Slide
- Slide 1: Introduce the topic and motivation
- Slide 2: Introduce the data
- Slides 3-5: Highlights from EDA
- Slide 6: Conclusions + discussion + critique

## Presentation

Presentations will be in person in lab. All team members must participate in the presentation. **The presentation must be no longer than 5 minutes.**

## Grading

The presentation is worth 20 points, broken down as follows:

| Total            | 20 pts |
|------------------|--------|
| **Slides**       | 10 pts |
| **Presentation** | 10 pts |

### Slides

- Organization and flow (3 pts): Slides follow a logical sequence, with clear titles and an easy-to-follow structure.

- Text and readability (3 pts): Text is concise, font sizes are readable, and slides avoid overcrowding.

- Figures and tables (3 pts): Figures and tables are easy to read, with legible labels, legends, and annotations.

- Consistency and polish (1 pt): Formatting is consistent throughout, with no distracting errors or layout issues.

### Presentation

- Time management (1 pt): The presentation is well paced and finishes within the time limit.

- Professionalism (1 pt): Delivery is clear and well-practiced.

- Teamwork (2 pts): The presentation tells a unified story, with smooth transitions between speakers and sections. Time is distributed among team members, and every team member contributes meaningfully.

- Creativity and critical thought (1 pt): The project demonstrates thoughtful planning, careful implementation, and intentional analytical choices.

- Research question and data suitability (1 pt): The research question is clearly articulated and can be answered with the data.

- Data (1 pt): The data and the key variables are clearly described.

- Analysis (1 pt): The analysis addresses the research question.

- Conclusions (1 pt): Conclusions are justified by the analysis.

- Limitations and/or future directions (1 pt): Relevant limitations and/or future directions are carefully considered and clearly articulated.
